What Makes Us Different

Built to move at the speed of the market

Conservation has traditionally moved at the pace of grants, committees, and consensus. Land markets move at the pace of capital. WAF was built to close that gap: a young, disruptive organization with the speed and financial sophistication of a private dealmaker, and the conservation experience of people who've spent their careers protecting America's most important landscapes.

Wild America Fund is not a competitor to existing land trusts, conservation transaction entities, or any other conservation organization. We're a force multiplier for them.

Today's land protection landscape is fast-moving and financially complex. Properties of high conservation value are often controlled by sophisticated sellers, attract multiple buyers, close on compressed timelines, and require capital structures that most conservation organizations aren't positioned to deploy alone. Deals get lost - not for lack of mission, but for lack of readiness.

WAF exists to close that gap. We bring ready capital to move quickly when a window opens, then design and orchestrate the broader capital strategy needed to get a deal across the finish line. We help assemble the right transactional expertise, expand the network of funders and partners, and hand off successfully protected land to the organizations best suited to steward it long-term, building their capacity along the way.

Every conservation organization can tell the story of the one that got away: the mission-critical property lost because conservation showed up undercapitalized and underprepared against staunch competition.

Think of us less as a dealmaker and more as a deal accelerator; the organization that gets conservation off the starting blocks to get deals over the finish line.

In the private sector, when a company needs to acquire another business, it doesn't go it alone - it calls in specialized support for the transaction. Not because the business lacks the intention or expertise, but because complex transactions require ready capital, strategic structuring, specialized expertise, and a network of relationships that accelerate and de-risk the process. Wild America Fund is that partner for land conservation.

Biodiversity challenge

Wildlife populations worldwide have declined by 73% since 1970, and one million species face extinction. In the United States, one of the world's most biodiverse countries, 34% of plant species and 40% of animal species are at risk.

Protecting intact ecosystems directly addresses the primary driver of biodiversity loss - habitat destruction - while strengthening climate resilience, enabling species migration, and securing public benefits like carbon sequestration and water purification.

Conservation sector challenge

Knowing where to act is no longer the hardest problem - science has mapped it. The harder problem is speed.

Conservation deals increasingly compete against private buyers and institutional capital in real estate markets that move on commercial timelines, not long philanthropic or public funding timelines. Without dedicated capital and dealmaking infrastructure ready to move the moment a window opens, the most important parcels are lost to those who can act faster.

Where We Work

Priority Biodiversity Landscapes Across the U.S.

WAF focuses on protecting the most scientifically critical landscapes before they are lost. Using current science on habitat connectivity, endemic species richness, and the conditions needed for durable protection, we have identified 16 priority regions across the continental United States.

Our Response

A disciplined national agenda

We expect to concentrate our earliest work in 6-8 of these regions, while retaining the flexibility to pursue high-priority opportunities elsewhere on our map as they arise because in real estate, readiness matters as much as location.

In applicable regions, our work engages Tribal partners as core partners in stewardship and protection.

Strategic Pillars

The four pillars of our work

WAF combines science, dealmaking, capital, and partner capacity to accelerate protection of the wild landscapes that matter most.

Pillar 01

Targeted conservation

WAF protects wild landscapes selected for their scientific biodiversity and climate resilience value. We have mapped a national agenda of 16 priority areas in the continental U.S. chosen for where habitat connectivity, species richness, and enabling conditions overlap to offer the strongest opportunities to protect biodiversity at scale.

Pillar 02

Expert dealmaking

Our staff bring deal structuring expertise that most conservation transactions today lack. Using WAF's biodiversity maps and the best available science down to the individual parcel, we develop clear acquisition criteria and partner with on-the-ground NGOs and agencies, bringing in specialized transactional support and capital to negotiate favorable terms and apply best practices across every deal to ensure conservation shows up as a formidable contender.

Pillar 03

Strategic financing

A dedicated capital fund sets WAF apart from how conservation has traditionally operated and sets us up to act proactively and on commercial timelines. We invest early to catalyze deals, then leverage that capital with matching commitments from public and philanthropic sources. Where possible, we recycle capital through sales to public agencies, stretching our funds across more transactions over time.

Pillar 04

Effective partnerships

WAF strengthens the conservation ecosystem rather than working around it. We bring our network of conservation, real estate, and philanthropic relationships, along with funding and dealmaking sophistication, to expand what is possible for local and national partners. We invest in partner NGOs' capacity, helping them manage protected properties for the long term and accelerate protection within their own goals and communities.

Case Study

Proof in Practice

In progress Santa Barbara County, California

Gaviota Coast - the beginning

A key component of one of WAF's 16 priority regions, selected for its high biodiversity value and readiness to succeed.

We have already begun work to protect our first region of focus: the Gaviota Coast of California, a key component of one of our 16 priority regions, selected for its high biodiversity value and readiness to succeed.

With preexisting protected lands and supportive local NGOs in this priority area, WAF's efforts can identify and acquire key private parcels for core habitat, corridor, and buffer protection - affording species migration and resilience.

The Gaviota Coast is a rugged 76-mile stretch of coastline and the largest remaining undeveloped coastal landscape in Southern California. Where the Pacific Ocean meets the mountains, this rare Mediterranean ecosystem supports exceptional biodiversity and serves as a refuge for iconic and imperiled species including southern steelhead, white-tailed kites, endangered pollinators, and California's native grasslands.

The Gaviota Coast serves as both a case study and a proving ground for our model: demonstrating how focused collaboration and a commitment to ecological integrity can deliver lasting, landscape-scale conservation.

In progress San Luis Obispo County, California

Wild Cherry Canyon - unlocking millions in state funding

A 2,400-acre flagship opportunity within the Diablo Canyon Lands on California's Central Coast.

We are advancing protection of the Wild Cherry Canyon property, adjacent to the Diablo Canyon Power Plant on California's Central Coast.

With $40 million in dedicated state funding, a committed acquiring partner in The Wildlands Conservancy, WAF's catalytic capital and dealmaking has secured this transaction at the speed the moment demands, establishing a model for protecting the additional 6,000-10,000 acres that surround it.

Wild Cherry Canyon is a mosaic of California's most pristine coastal prairie, ancient oak woodland, chaparral, and rare bishop pine forest, ranked highest in The Nature Conservancy's California Wild Coast assessment. Its 14 miles of undeveloped shoreline and intact tide pools shelter threatened and ecologically vital species including South-Central coast steelhead, California red-legged frogs, western burrowing owls, and the American badger.

Wild Cherry Canyon serves as both a case study and a proving ground for our model: demonstrating how catalytic capital and aligned partnership can move quickly enough to secure a rare, time-sensitive opportunity and unlock the public funding needed to protect an entire landscape.

Team

Nature's new acquisition team

A young organization, led by a veteran team. The Wild America Fund is founded and led by Kim Elliman, Michael Mantell, and Michael Bell, who serves as founding CEO: three of the most respected dealmakers in American conservation, who came together to build something that did not exist yet: an organization with the speed and capital discipline to compete for the most important land before it is too late.

Kim Elliman

Christopher (Kim) Elliman is a co-founder and advisor of the Wild America Fund. Kim retired as CEO of the Open Space Institute in 2024, after 32 years with OSI. In those years, in over 1000 transactions, OSI conserved more than 2.5 million acres in the eastern United States and created or enhanced over 600 parks and protected lands.

Dodge Foundation; Overhills Foundation; Wild Wings Foundation; Samuel Freeman Charitable Trust; The Wilderness Society; Grow NYC; and as a trustee of the Wildlife Conservation Society; Black Rock Forest Preserve; Storm King Art Center; Center for Humans and Nature; the Dalton School, and on numerous advisory boards and investment committees. Mr. Elliman received his B.A. from Yale University and now serves on the Leadership Council Steering Committee of its School of the Environment.

Mr. Elliman was an award-winning journalist, acted in an Academy Award winning documentary, and worked in NYC government and in renewable energy early in his career. Mr. Elliman has received numerous awards for his work in conservation from Black Rock Forest, GrowNYC, Open Space Institute, Parks and Trails NY, the Adirondack Council, Palisades Park Conservancy, among others.

Mr. Elliman lives in NYC and has four grown children, two granddaughters, and is married to Jennifer Cunningham.

Co-founder | CEO

Michael Bell

Michael Bell is the co-founder and CEO of Wild America Fund. Prior to founding Wild America Fund, Michael spent 26 years at The Nature Conservancy (TNC), most recently serving as Director of Protection for California. In that role, he led a team of transactional specialists managing a large and complex pipeline of conservation projects across and beyond California, with a portfolio exceeding $1 billion. Over his tenure, he cultivated an extensive network of donors and partners and completed more than 100 conservation transactions.

In 2017, Michael led the negotiation and fundraising effort to acquire and permanently protect the 24,460-acre Point Conception property—one of the most significant coastal conservation achievements in California history. The project was enabled by a $165 million gift from Jack and Laura Dangermond, the largest in TNC’s history. Following the acquisition, he led the design and fundraising for the Point Conception Institute, an open-data, collaborative research center that partners with institutions including NASA, the Smithsonian, and National Geographic.

Earlier, Michael served as Director of Oceans for TNC’s California Chapter, where he led a multidisciplinary team advancing innovative, transaction-based solutions to coastal and ocean challenges. Working closely with industry and government, he helped pioneer fisheries reform approaches that contributed to the recovery and long-term sustainability of the West Coast groundfish fishery and have informed similar efforts globally. He has co-authored several scientific publications on fisheries co-management and conservation innovation.

Michael joined TNC in California in 2003 as Project Director for San Luis Obispo and Santa Barbara Counties, following his role as Director of Protection for TNC’s New Jersey Chapter. Earlier in his career, he served in the Peace Corps in Uruguay and conducted research on small-scale fisheries in La Paz, Baja California Sur, Mexico.

He holds a B.S. in Biology from Saint Anselm College and lives in Los Osos, California with his wife, daughter, son, and dog.
